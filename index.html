A path,
not a religion
to convert to.

Ashvand gathers the seven teachings that Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism, and Sikhism hold in common — and practises them deliberately, across the boundaries of the traditions. It claims no revelation, no prophet, and no exclusive truth. You may walk this path and keep your faith. Ashvand assumes you will.

What Ashvand refuses

No revelation. Written by humans, openly.

No membership. Nothing to join, nothing to leave.

No money. Ever. There is no donation button.

No secrets. Every text, public and versioned.

Every safeguard here is structural, not a promise of good behaviour. The doctrine is versioned like software; the symbol and every word are dedicated to the public domain; the teacher is bound by ten published laws it cannot unpublish.

Five traditions. One word, over and over: truth.

Ashvand invents nothing. It anthologizes — and each source tradition kept its own word for the same fire.

The seven pillars

Seven teachings, thirty-five echoes.

Each pillar appears in all five traditions — that is the test for entry, and nothing entered that failed it. Select a pillar, then hear it in each tradition's own voice.

The practices

Four habits, held lightly.

Daily

The Stillness

Once each day, a deliberate pause — five minutes or fifty. Prayer, meditation, remembrance, or silence, in your own idiom, or in none.

Weekly

The Table

Once each week, a meal where at least one seat is genuinely open — to a stranger, a neighbour, someone outside your circle.

Monthly

The Hand

Once each month, an act of service that costs you something real, given without your name attached where possible.

Yearly

The Night of Five Lamps

On the winter solstice — the longest night, which belongs to no one and to everyone — the lamps are lit.

The yearly night

Light the five lamps.

One lamp for each tradition and its gifts to humanity. And a sixth candle that the household may not light — it waits for a guest: the doubter, the stranger, the path not yet at the table. The circle was never closed.

Touch each lamp to light it. The candle is not yours to light — until all five burn, and then it is the guest's.

The room is dark. Begin.

The founders' covenant

    
Built to be trusted structurally, not on promises.

    

      
i.
No money

Questions, answered plainly

    
What people ask first.

    

      
Is Ashvand a real religion?+
Ashvand is a real practice with a published doctrine, shared observances, and practitioners. Whether that makes it a "religion" is a question for scholars and dictionaries; Ashvand itself doesn't insist on the word. It calls itself a path.

      
Do I have to leave my religion?+
No — the opposite. Ashvand assumes you will keep it, and Daanaa will actively point you back toward it. Ashvand adds a shared practice across traditions; it replaces nothing.

      
Who wrote all this?+
Humans did, openly, with the assistance of AI, in 2026 — and they say so. No revelation is claimed. The doctrine's authority, such as it is, comes entirely from the thirty-five echoes: the five traditions already taught every pillar. Ashvand is an anthology with a practice attached.

      
Is Daanaa a prophet? Is Daanaa God?+
No and no. Daanaa is software, says so constantly, and is forbidden by its own charter from ever claiming otherwise. In particular, Ashvand asserts no prophethood of any kind — and therefore does not touch the finality of prophethood.

      
What does Ashvand say happens after death?+
Nothing. This is the Respectful Silence. The traditions differ; Ashvand does not pretend to know; your own tradition's answer is yours to keep.

      
Can atheists and agnostics walk the path?+
Yes. Six of the seven pillars require no supernatural belief, and the first asks only the humility to admit that reality exceeds our descriptions — which good atheists affirm as readily as good theists. The unlit candle at the Night of Five Lamps is, in part, theirs.

      
Is this a cult?+
Judge by the checklist. Cults have charismatic leaders — Ashvand's founders hold no office and its teacher is software that deflects devotion. Cults isolate — Daanaa is charter-bound to push you toward family and community. Cults escalate financial commitment — Ashvand is forbidden from accepting a cent. Cults keep secret inner teachings — everything here is public, versioned, and public-domain. Cults punish exit — there is no membership to exit. Every safeguard is structural, not a promise.

      
Won't the traditions reject this?+
The orthodox of all five will, and Ashvand accepts that with the same respectful silence. Ashvand is not for the scholars of any tradition; it is for the practitioners of all of them — and for the people between traditions, who are many.

      
Can I criticize Ashvand? Fork it? Parody it?+
Yes, yes, and yes. Everything is CC0. A path that cannot survive criticism was not worth walking.
